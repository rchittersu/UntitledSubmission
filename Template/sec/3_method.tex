\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

Given a native-resolution defocused image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}$, an existing deblurring
method $\mathcal{D}$ is applied to its $4\times$ downscaled version and yields the \emph{anchor}
$a = \mathcal{D}(x{\downarrow_4})$, which has the structure of the all-in-focus image but no native
detail. \method produces the native all-in-focus estimate $\hat{y}$ from $x$, $a$ and a blur map
$b$ that gives the local amount of defocus. It operates on tiles $T$ of $x$ with overlap and
border-aware blending, but every tile has access to a memory $\mathcal{M}$ built once per image:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}_T = g_T \odot x_T + (1-g_T) \odot G\big(x_T,\, a{\uparrow}_T,\, b_T;\, \mathcal{M}\big),
  \label{eq:output}
\end{equation}
where $a{\uparrow}$ is the bicubically upsampled anchor, $G$ is the generator (\cref{sec:variants})
and $g$ a learned copy gate.

\subsection{Copy path and blur map}
\label{sec:copy}
In focal-plane regions the observation \emph{is} the answer, and any re-synthesis can only damage it
(\cref{sec:exp_results}). The gate $g$ is predicted from the blur map and the features of $G$ and
initialized to the in-focus probability. The blur map $b$ measures the local defocus in pixels. When
dual-pixel views are available it is obtained from their disparity, which is proportional to the
signed defocus; we use this as an oracle. Otherwise $b$ is predicted by a small head from the
agreement between the anchor and the downscaled observation: re-blurring a faithful anchor with the
correct local kernel reproduces the observation~\cite{zhuo2011defocusmap}, so the best-fitting kernel
radius estimates the defocus that was removed, and the residual flags unreliable anchors.

\subsection{In-focus exemplar memory}
\label{sec:memory}
Inside a defocused tile, the observation carries no native detail, and the anchor carries none above
its own Nyquist frequency. The memory supplies it from the in-focus parts of the whole image.
\emph{Construction.} Native cells of $64\times64$ pixels whose blur map indicates focus, and whose
downscaled version agrees with the anchor, are ranked by texture energy; the top $K$ form the memory,
optionally at several scales to account for perspective. Each cell $j$ has a key $k_j$, computed by an
encoder $E_k$ from its $4\times$ downscaled version, and a value $v_j$, a set of tokens computed by an
encoder $E_v$ from the native cell.
\emph{Retrieval.} Keys live in the anchor domain: the downscaled in-focus observation and the anchor
are both sharp at low resolution, so a defocused region and its in-focus counterpart elsewhere have
similar keys even though their native observations differ completely. A tile queries the memory with
$q = E_k(a_T)$ and attends to its top-$k$ cells and a learned null entry,
\begin{equation}
  r = \operatorname{softmax}\!\big(q^\top [k_{j_1},\dots,k_{j_k}, k_\varnothing] / \tau\big),
  \label{eq:relevance}
\end{equation}
which yields the transferred features $\sum_i r_i v_{j_i}$ and a relevance map $\max_i r_i$ used for
gating and analysis. The null entry lets $G$ fall back to its prior where nothing relevant is in focus.
During training the memory is built from the whole image while the loss is computed on a crop, and
cells overlapping the crop are dropped with probability $0.5$ so that the model learns to transfer
rather than copy in place.

\subsection{Generator variants}
\label{sec:variants}
\paragraph{Feed-forward.} A U-Net with NAFNet-style blocks~\cite{chen2022nafnet} takes
$[x_T, a{\uparrow}_T, b_T]$ and predicts a residual over $a{\uparrow}_T$; memory features enter through
cross-attention at $1/4$ and $1/8$ resolution. It is trained with $\ell_1$, perceptual and frequency
losses and, in a second stage, a light adversarial loss restricted to defocused pixels.
\paragraph{One-step diffusion transformer.} A pretrained latent diffusion
transformer~\cite{peebles2023dit,esser2024sd3} is adapted with LoRA to predict the clean latent in
one step from the noised anchor latent, conditioned on the latents of $x_T$ and on $b_T$; memory
tokens are appended to the joint-attention context. Since a VAE does not reproduce in-focus native
texture exactly, the copy path of \cref{eq:output} stays in pixel space.

\subsection{Training data and plug-in anchors}
\label{sec:training}
We train on DPDD at native resolution ($350$ scenes, \cref{sec:exp_data}). Anchors are produced by a
pool of existing deblurring methods and by simulation (the downscaled target degraded by residual blur
proportional to $b$, noise and ringing), which prevents overfitting to one method; the published
methods were trained on DPDD at the anchor resolution, so their training-set anchors are better than
their test-set anchors, and the simulated anchors compensate. Held-out methods test the plug-in claim.
